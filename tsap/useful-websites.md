The **Useful Websites** page is a growing list of websites and online tools that we explore for their potential usefulness in the work of The Sunil Abraham Project (TSAP). These resources may support research, website development, coding, digital preservation, cybersecurity, automation, data analysis, and other project activities.

The list focuses primarily on specialised tools, useful directories and websites that may be less familiar to our readers, with practical benefits for TSAP. We have deliberately excluded many widely known general-purpose websites, as the aim is to discover and document resources that may otherwise go unnoticed.

Sections and entries within each section are arranged alphabetically.

## Artificial Intelligence {#artificial-intelligence}
1. [AI News Hub](https://aienews.org/), aggregates AI-related updates from multiple sources, including research labs, open-source projects and AI companies. Useful for scanning developments across the AI ecosystem in one place; check the linked sources for context and verify how frequently the feed is updated.
2. [Artificial Analysis](https://artificialanalysis.ai/), tracks and compares AI models using evaluations and measurements such as model performance, speed and pricing. Useful for comparing models and checking how new releases perform beyond their providers' own claims.
3. [ArtificialWatch](https://artificialwatch.com/), tracks AI model releases and provides structured information about availability, release announcements and API pricing. Its release log can help distinguish API availability from an official announcement, while its data feeds support ongoing monitoring.
4. [Onei AI](https://onei.ai/), provides a searchable directory of AI apps, agent skills and Model Context Protocol (MCP) servers. Its categories and filters help discover AI tools and integrations, including resources for coding agents and assistants.
5. [Toolify.ai](https://www.toolify.ai/), is a searchable directory of AI tools and services, organised by categories such as writing, image generation, video, coding, productivity and research. Useful for discovering specialised tools that may otherwise be difficult to find. Its listings and popularity rankings are not independent quality assessments, and some placements are paid; verify each tool's pricing, privacy practices and capabilities on its official website.

## Data Protection and Privacy Law {#data-protection}
1. [Centre for Applied Data Protection](https://cadp.in/), an India-focused research centre working on data protection law and policy, privacy jurisprudence and implementation of the Digital Personal Data Protection Act. Explore its publications and research outputs for legal and policy analysis.
2. [DSCI Data Protection Framework](https://www.dsci.in/content/data-protection-framework-india), provides resources on India's data protection framework, including analysis of the Digital Personal Data Protection Rules, 2025, thematic FAQs and legal developments. Useful for tracking implementation and examining the legal and policy implications of India's data protection regime.
3. [GDPRhub](https://gdprhub.eu/), maintains a searchable collection of summaries of decisions by European data protection authorities and courts, together with legal analysis. Useful for comparative research into regulatory enforcement and the interpretation of privacy law.
4. [PrivacyLawHub](https://privacylawhub.com/), provides legal research resources on India's DPDP Act and Rules, the GDPR and other privacy laws, including regulator guidance and policy materials. Useful for researching data protection legislation and comparing legal frameworks.

## Data Repositories and Dataset Discovery {#data-repositories}
1. [Google Dataset Search](https://datasetsearch.research.google.com/), helps researchers discover datasets published across universities, research institutions, government portals and other repositories. Search results link to the original sources, where you can inspect available formats, metadata, access conditions and licences before using a dataset. It is a discovery tool rather than a dataset host, so availability and reuse terms depend on the source.
2. [ORBIT](https://orbit-research.app/dataset-discovery/), helps researchers discover datasets across repositories such as Zenodo, Figshare and Dryad, as well as machine-learning datasets on Hugging Face. It provides information about licences and reuse conditions and supports exporting search results and citations. ORBIT links to the original dataset rather than hosting the files, and some datasets may have access restrictions; its free tier has limited searches and requires an account.

## Digital Accessibility Research and Tools {#digital-accessibility}
1. [Certvo Accessibility Research](https://certvo.com/research), publishes web accessibility research and downloadable JSON and CSV datasets documenting automated accessibility checks against WCAG criteria, including colour contrast, missing alternative text and inaccessible links. Its 2026 report covers 1,569 websites and 4,562 pages. The accompanying methodology explains the sampling approach and its limitations: the sample is weighted towards commercial websites, and automated checks cannot assess every aspect of accessibility. Treat the findings as a research dataset, not a representative measure of the entire web.

## Digital Rights and Internet Law {#digital-rights-law}
1. [CYRILLA](https://cyrilla.org/), provides a searchable collection of laws, court decisions and other legal resources concerning human rights in digital environments. Useful for comparing how countries regulate digital rights, online expression and access to information.
2. [Digital Constitutionalism Network](https://digitalconstitutionalism.org/), maintains a research database of declarations, bills of rights, charters and laws addressing internet-related rights and principles of digital governance. Useful for tracing how digital rights are articulated in legal and policy frameworks.
3. [Global Network Initiative - Country Legal Frameworks Resource](https://clfr.globalnetworkinitiative.org/), provides country-level information on legal frameworks governing communications surveillance, access to communications data and online content restrictions. Useful for research into privacy, freedom of expression and government powers over digital communications.
4. [World Intermediary Liability Map (WILMap)](https://wilmap.stanford.edu/), documents laws, court decisions and policy developments relating to online intermediaries around the world. Useful for comparative research into platform liability, content moderation and freedom of expression.

## GitHub and Repository Tools {#github}
1. [ExplainGitHub](https://explaingithub.com/), provides an AI-powered workspace for understanding a repository through code-aware questions, onboarding reports, and architecture exploration. [Open the TSAP repository in ExplainGitHub](https://explaingithub.com/sunilabrahamindia/sunilabraham).
2. [GitDiagram](https://gitdiagram.com/), generates an interactive architecture diagram of a GitHub repository. [View the TSAP repository diagram](https://gitdiagram.com/sunilabrahamindia/sunilabraham).
3. [GitHub1s](https://github1s.com/), opens a GitHub repository in a browser-based, VS Code-like interface for navigating and searching source files. [Open the TSAP repository in GitHub1s](https://github1s.com/sunilabrahamindia/sunilabraham).
4. [GitIngest](https://gitingest.com/), converts a GitHub repository into a text digest that can be used for AI-assisted code analysis. [Generate a digest of the TSAP repository](https://gitingest.com/sunilabrahamindia/sunilabraham).
5. [GitMCP](https://gitmcp.io/), provides an MCP endpoint that lets compatible AI assistants retrieve documentation and code from GitHub repositories to help answer project-specific questions. [Open the TSAP repository's GitMCP endpoint](https://gitmcp.io/sunilabrahamindia/sunilabraham).
6. [GitReverse](https://gitreverse.com/), analyses repository information to generate an AI prompt that can help recreate a similar project. [Try GitReverse with the TSAP repository](https://gitreverse.com/sunilabrahamindia/sunilabraham).
7. [Octotree](https://www.octotree.io/), adds a file-tree interface to GitHub repositories to make browsing source code easier. [Browse the TSAP repository on GitHub](https://github.com/sunilabrahamindia/sunilabraham) with the Octotree browser extension.
8. [Uithub](https://uithub.com/), turns a GitHub repository into structured, AI-friendly context, with options to filter files and control the output format or token budget. [Open the TSAP repository in Uithub](https://uithub.com/sunilabrahamindia/sunilabraham). Check its current sign-in requirements and permissions before sharing private repository content.

## Images and Mapping {#images-and-mapping}
1. [EXIF.im](https://exif.im/), an online image metadata viewer that helps inspect EXIF information such as camera details, capture time and, when present, GPS coordinates. Review metadata before publishing photographs, since location data may reveal sensitive information.
2. [uMap](https://umap.openstreetmap.fr/), lets you create and share interactive maps using OpenStreetMap data. Maps can include markers, descriptions and layers, and can be embedded in webpages to document places and local points of interest.

## Internet Governance and Policy {#internet-governance}
1. [Civis](https://www.civis.vote/consultations/list), tracks public consultations on draft laws, rules, policies and government proposals in India. It lists consultations and helps people understand proposals and submit feedback while consultations are open. Useful for finding policies at the public consultation stage.
2. [Digital Watch Observatory](https://dig.watch/), organises information about digital policy issues, stakeholders, international governance processes, events and policy instruments. Its policy resources can help researchers trace how institutions and processes shape global digital governance.
3. [PolicyDhara](https://policydhara.impactmojo.in/), tracks Indian public policy proposals and developments, bringing together consultation drafts, parliamentary bills, parliamentary questions and official documents. Useful for following policies in the making and tracing proposals through different stages; consult the original documents to verify their wording and current status.
4. [UNIDIR Cyber Policy Portal Database](https://database.cyberpolicyportal.org/), provides searchable national cyber policy documents and related resources. Useful for comparing cybersecurity strategies, international frameworks and approaches to cyber diplomacy across countries.
5. [World Map of Encryption Laws and Policies](https://www.gp-digital.org/world-map-of-encryption/), by Global Partners Digital, compares laws, policies and practices affecting encryption across 100 countries. It assesses both restrictions on strong encryption and safeguards against abuse of government powers. Country entries identify the assessment framework and date, which is important when using the map for comparative research.
6. [World Monitor](https://www.worldmonitor.app/), combines global news feeds with interactive maps and data on geopolitical developments, conflicts, cyber threats, internet infrastructure, shipping, aviation and economic indicators. Useful for monitoring international developments and exploring connections between events across different regions and sectors. The dashboard is free to use, and the source code is available under the AGPL-3.0 licence. Verify important findings against original sources, as aggregated data and automated analysis may be incomplete or inaccurate.

## Internet Measurement and Infrastructure {#internet-measurement}
1. [Cloudflare Radar - Outage Center](https://radar.cloudflare.com/outage-center), provides a global view of reported internet outages and unusual traffic patterns, with information about affected locations and time periods. Useful for investigating connectivity disruptions and comparing regional patterns over time. The data should be interpreted in the context of the source's methodology and coverage.
2. [Internet Society Pulse](https://pulse.internetsociety.org/), brings together indicators on internet resilience, infrastructure, outages, market concentration and other aspects of internet health. Country comparisons and data can support policy briefs and comparative studies.
3. [IODA - Internet Outage Detection and Analysis](https://ioda.inetintel.cc.gatech.edu/), combines network signals to help detect and investigate large-scale internet connectivity disruptions. Historical views can support research into outages and possible shutdown events; the measurements need to be interpreted alongside other evidence.
4. [ip-as.net Research](https://ip-as.net/research/), provides research datasets on internet infrastructure and security, including hosting, DNS, email security, IPv6, route security and phishing listings. Its datasets may be useful for technical and policy research; check the publication date and licence for the specific dataset before reuse.
5. [RIPEstat](https://stat.ripe.net/), provides data about IP addresses, autonomous systems, routing and internet number resources. Useful for investigating network relationships, routing history and aspects of internet infrastructure.

## Internet Shutdowns and Censorship {#shutdowns-censorship}
1. [Internet Shutdowns Tracker](https://internetshutdowns.in/india-shutdowns/), documents internet shutdowns in India, with event information that can be examined by location and time. Useful for research into connectivity restrictions and their policy implications; check the linked sources and methodology when citing individual events.
2. [OONI Explorer](https://explorer.ooni.org/), lets researchers explore published network measurements that may indicate website or app blocking. Measurements can be filtered by country, network, date and test type; individual results require careful interpretation and corroboration.

## JavaScript Libraries and Tools {#javascript-libraries}

1. [UnsuckJS](https://unsuckjs.com/), curates lightweight JavaScript and CSS libraries (generally around 10 KB or less) that can enhance ordinary HTML without build tools or compilers. Its comparison table includes compressed file size, licence, latest commit, version and other project details, helping developers assess small libraries before using them.

## Knowledge Management and Learning {#knowledge-management-learning}

Platforms, directories and tools that support structured learning, independent study, knowledge discovery, skill development and the organisation of learning resources.
1. [AIStudyTools](https://www.aistudytools.com/), offers self-paced courses with lessons, explanatory notes, worked examples, interactive learning tools and final assessments. Its topics include statistics, prompt engineering, product management, HR analytics, building AI agents and coding for non-coders. The platform advertises its listed courses as free and offers certificates after passing the final test; check the course page for current terms.
2. [AITeky Learn](https://learn.aiteky.com/), brings together a large catalogue of learning resources from universities, companies and other providers, alongside structured skill packs, job-role learning paths and a directory of courses advertised with free certificates. It covers AI, machine learning, programming, data, cloud, cybersecurity and other technical subjects. Many resources and listed certificates are free, but some lessons require an account or programme access, and the site also offers paid training; check the original provider's conditions before enrolling or relying on a certificate.
3. [Belinkee](https://www.belinkee.com/), offers career-based learning roadmaps and a catalogue of courses covering topics such as programming, AI, cybersecurity, databases and infrastructure. Its roadmaps connect stages of learning with lessons and courses; check which materials are free and which require payment.
4. [Class Central](https://www.classcentral.com/), helps learners discover and compare online courses from universities, technology companies and other education providers. Its catalogue, subject guides and learner reviews can help identify courses across a wide range of fields. Many courses offer free access or auditing, while certificates and some course features may require payment; check the original provider's terms before enrolling.
5. [Codédex](https://www.codedex.io/), offers beginner-friendly, interactive programming courses and project tutorials covering Python, HTML, CSS, JavaScript, SQL, Git and GitHub, and other technology topics. Its game-like learning format uses exercises, experience points and badges to encourage practice, while project guides help learners build practical skills. Many introductory lessons are free, although some courses and features require a paid membership.
6. [Dantes](https://dantes.io/), organises learning resources across a wide range of subjects and topics, including books, courses, videos and other materials. Its curated learning paths can help learners move from introductory material to more advanced study.
7. [DevRoadmaps](https://rudra496.github.io/devroadmaps/), is an open-source project offering structured developer learning paths, curated resources and certification guidance. It may help learners organise their study, although the project's maintenance and the quality of individual resources should be checked.
8. [Educypedia](https://educypedia.org/), is a broad directory of educational links covering subjects such as computing, electronics, science, engineering and history. It is useful for discovering reference materials and learning resources across many disciplines.
9. [Learn Anything](https://learn-anything.xyz/), helps users explore connections between topics through knowledge maps and curated learning paths. Useful for discovering learning resources, understanding how concepts relate to one another, and navigating unfamiliar subjects. The project is open source.
10. [OpenSemester](https://opensemester.github.io/), is an open-source initiative focused on undergraduate engineering and computer science learning, bringing together structured study modules, academic resources and capstone projects. It is more specialised than general-purpose learning directories.
11. [roadmap.sh](https://roadmap.sh/), provides community-curated, interactive roadmaps, study plans and learning resources for technology skills and careers. Topics include programming, web development, Python, AI, cybersecurity, DevOps, data science and system design. Each roadmap helps learners identify relevant skills, understand their learning sequence and explore linked resources. Useful for planning structured learning paths and identifying skills to develop.
12. [SkillVeris](https://www.skillveris.com/), offers structured technology courses and career learning paths across AI and machine learning, programming, web development, cloud, DevOps, security and databases. Its 24/7 AI tutor (called AI Mentor) answers learners' questions, while assessments, coding practice and study notes support self-paced learning. The platform states that its courses, AI tutor and certificates are free, with no subscription or paywall; verify certificate recognition independently before relying on it for formal credentials.
13. [TutorAI](https://tutorai.me/), generates personalised courses on topics chosen by the learner, with structured lessons, quizzes, study materials and AI-assisted homework help. It can be useful for exploring unfamiliar subjects through a guided learning path. TutorAI is not completely free: it offers a limited free tier alongside paid subscription plans. Verify AI-generated explanations against reliable sources, as lessons and answers may contain errors.
14. [YUVA AI for ALL](https://www.futureskillsprime.in/course/yuva-ai-for-all/), is a free, self-paced introductory AI course launched by India's Ministry of Electronics and Information Technology under the IndiaAI Mission. The 4.5-hour course introduces AI concepts, real-world applications and responsible AI use through six modules. Learners who complete it receive an official Government of India certificate. Check the course page for enrolment and completion details.

## Online Code Editors and Compilers {#online-code-editors}
1. [OneCompiler](https://onecompiler.com/), an online code editor and compiler that lets you write, run, and test code in multiple programming languages without installing them locally.

## Online Tools and Utilities {#online-tools-utilities}
1. [delphitools](https://delphi.tools/), is a free, open-source collection of browser-based utilities for image editing and conversion, colour and accessibility checks, audio and video processing, PDF preparation, typography, text, QR codes, and developer tasks. The site states that it has no logins, paywalls, tracking, or data collection, and that tools process data locally in the browser. Its source code is released under the permissive 0BSD licence. As with any browser tool, check whether a particular feature needs an internet connection before relying on it offline.

## Open Educational Resources {#open-educational-resources}
1. [Open Textbook Library](https://open.umn.edu/opentextbooks/), provides free, peer-reviewed, openly licensed textbooks across a range of academic subjects. Many titles can be downloaded, shared and adapted, making the library useful for teaching, independent study and finding reusable educational material. Check the licence on each book before adapting or redistributing it.

## Platform Accountability and Content Removal {#platform-accountability}
1. [Lumen Database](https://lumendatabase.org/), collects and analyses notices and requests to remove online content, including copyright complaints and requests involving defamation, privacy and other legal claims. Its records can help researchers examine how legal demands affect the availability of online material. Coverage depends on notices shared with the database, so it should not be treated as a complete record of all removals.
2. [Ranking Digital Rights](https://rankingdigitalrights.org/), publishes research assessing technology and telecommunications companies on policies affecting privacy, freedom of expression and governance. Its company comparisons and methodology can support research into platform transparency and corporate accountability.

## Python Development Resources {#python}
1. [Awesome Python](https://awesome-python.com/), a searchable, categorised guide to Python frameworks, libraries and tools, covering areas such as AI, web development, data analysis, automation, testing and security.

## Research Papers and Policy Publications {#research-publications}
1. [Global Data Justice](https://www.globaldatajustice.org/gdj/category/resources/publications/), publishes research on data governance, digital rights and social justice, including work on how data systems affect communities and public life. Useful for exploring critical perspectives on data-driven policy and digital governance.
2. [Internet Governance Project - Research](https://www.internetgovernance.org/research/), shares research and publications on internet governance, digital institutions, infrastructure and the political economy of digital networks. Useful for examining the institutions and processes shaping the global internet.
3. [Internet Policy Review](https://policyreview.info/publications), an open-access academic journal publishing research on internet regulation, digital policy and the social implications of digital technologies. Its publication archive is useful for finding scholarly articles and policy-relevant analysis.
4. [PolicyArchive](https://www.policyarchive.org/), a searchable archive of public policy research and reports from policy organisations and research institutions. Useful for discovering policy-focused publications beyond academic journals, including reports that can inform research on digital rights, internet governance and public policy.
5. [Semantic Scholar](https://www.semanticscholar.org/), a free, AI-powered academic search engine for discovering scholarly papers across many research fields. It provides citation links, related-paper recommendations, and available full-text links; paper summaries, alerts and library features are available for supported papers and accounts. Useful for finding and tracing research relevant to internet governance, digital rights and technology policy.

## Science Education and Simulations {#science-education-simulations}
1. [JavaLab](https://javalab.org/en/), offers free, interactive, browser-based science simulations covering physics, chemistry, astronomy, Earth science, biology and mathematics. The simulations run on desktop and mobile devices without requiring membership, making the site useful for visualising scientific concepts and exploring virtual experiments. Check individual simulations for their assumptions and limitations before using them for formal teaching or research.

## Self-Hosting and Open-Source Software {#self-hosting}
1. [Awesome Selfhosted](https://awesome-selfhosted.net/), a curated directory of free software and web applications that can be hosted on your own servers. Useful for discovering self-hosted alternatives for collaboration, documentation, automation, privacy and digital preservation.
2. [BuildForPeople](https://buildforpeople.dev/), is a directory of open-source and low-profit software, with information about licensing, funding models and project maturity. Useful for finding tools that prioritise user interests and for comparing alternatives to commercial services; check each project's current licence, maintenance and funding details.
3. [OpenSourceSwap](https://opensourceswap.com/), helps users discover open-source alternatives to commercial software. Its listings link to project repositories and provide information such as licences and recent activity, which can help with initial comparisons; verify the project's current maintenance, features and licence before adopting it.
4. [SelfHostHub](https://selfhosthub.dev/), is a directory for discovering self-hostable software across categories such as communication, development, automation, analytics and file management. Useful for finding tools to run on your own server; check each project's documentation, maintenance status and licence before deploying.
5. [There's an OS for That](https://theresanosforthat.com/), is a directory for discovering open-source software and alternatives to proprietary services, including self-hosting platforms and server tools. Useful for exploring options before choosing software; verify project activity, licensing and deployment requirements.

## System Administration, Networking and Security {#system-administration}
1. [crt.sh](https://crt.sh/), searches public TLS certificate transparency records. It can help researchers inspect certificate history and discover subdomains associated with a domain.
2. [The Book of Secret Knowledge](https://github.com/trimstray/the-book-of-secret-knowledge), a curated collection of command-line tools, networking resources, system administration utilities, security references, tutorials and technical guides.

## Text Analysis and Research {#text-analysis}
1. [Voyant Tools](https://voyant-tools.org/), a browser-based text analysis environment for exploring a collection of articles, speeches or other documents. It helps reveal word frequencies, recurring phrases, changes in vocabulary and relationships between terms through interactive visualisations. Useful for analysing written material as part of research and documentation.

## Web Design and UI Components {#web-design-ui-components}
1. [React Bits](https://www.reactbits.dev/), is a library of customisable animated React components, including text effects, interactive elements, backgrounds and micro-interactions. Live previews and reusable code make it useful for exploring interface ideas; components are intended for React projects and may need adaptation for non-React websites.
2. [Uiverse](https://uiverse.io/), a community-built library of reusable interface elements such as buttons, cards, loaders, forms and toggles. Components can be copied in formats including HTML/CSS, Tailwind and React, with Figma support. The site states that its UI elements are released under the MIT License.

## Web Research and Development {#web-research}

1. [Can I Use](https://caniuse.com/), a browser-compatibility reference for checking support for HTML, CSS and JavaScript features across desktop and mobile browsers. Useful for checking whether a feature used in an interactive page is supported by visitors' browsers and whether a fallback may be needed before publishing.
2. [Cheatography](https://cheatography.com/), offers user-created cheat sheets and quick reference guides covering programming, software, education, business and other subjects. Useful for quick look-ups, but the quality and currency of entries vary, so verify important information against reliable sources. The site can display a substantial amount of advertising, which may interrupt browsing.
3. [PublicWWW](https://publicwww.com/), a source-code search engine for discovering websites that contain particular HTML snippets, scripts, widgets or tracking codes. Useful for web research, investigating website implementations and discovering how specific technologies are used across the web.
4. [QuickRef.ME](https://quickref.me/), provides concise cheat sheets and quick references for programming languages, web technologies, Git, Linux commands, databases and other developer tools. Useful for quickly checking syntax and common commands without searching through full documentation; verify details against official documentation when accuracy or version-specific behaviour matters.
5. [Transform](https://transform.tools/) is a free, open-source collection of developer-focused converters for transforming data and code between formats. It can convert JSON into TypeScript types, Go structs, Rust types and Zod schemas, SVG into JSX, CSS into JavaScript objects or Tailwind CSS, and HTML into JSX. Useful for reducing repetitive coding and converting data structures without installing separate tools. Review generated output before using it in production.
6. [urlscan.io](https://urlscan.io/), analyses a submitted URL in an automated browser and reports details such as a screenshot, network requests, loaded resources and connected domains. Useful for investigating website behaviour and external connections. Public scans can expose the submitted URL and results, so avoid submitting private or sensitive links.
