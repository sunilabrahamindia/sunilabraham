The **Internet and Free Knowledge in India** (IFKI) is a TSAP initiative to document selected parts of the history and development of the Internet and free knowledge movement in India. The subject is large and spans many people, organisations, projects, technologies, movements, events, and publications, and this project will not attempt to cover all of them. It will instead focus on subjects that can be meaningfully documented from available sources and material encountered during the project.

The project currently begins with a reading list of books and other publications, which will help identify subjects that may be worth documenting separately.

## Books

- *The Digital Decades: Thirty Years of the Internet in India* — Subimal Bhattacharjee
- *The Maverick Effect: The Inside Story of India's IT Revolution* — Harish Mehta. ISBN: 978-9352829966
- *FOSS is Necessary but not Sufficient: Lessons from the History and Politics of Free and Open-Source Software Movements in India* — Jasmine Folz
- *Free Culture: How Big Media Uses Technology and the Law to Lock Down Culture and Control Creativity* — Lawrence Lessig. ISBN: 978-1583225820
- *The Wealth of Networks: How Social Production Transforms Markets and Freedom* — Yochai Benkler. ISBN: 978-0300110562
- *The Cathedral and the Bazaar: Musings on Linux and Open Source by an Accidental Revolutionary* — Eric S. Raymond. ISBN: 978-0596001087

Other books and publications may be added, particularly material concerning the Kerala free software movement, Indian-language computing, digital libraries, open access, Creative Commons, and Wikimedia in India.

## Scope

The initial scope includes:

- Early computer networks and the Internet in India
- ERNET and academic networking
- The growth of public Internet access
- India's software and information technology history
- Free and open-source software
- The free software movement in India, including Kerala
- Indian-language computing
- Digital libraries and digitisation
- Open access and scholarly communication
- Creative Commons
- Wikipedia and Wikimedia in India
- Internet governance and digital rights
- Digital preservation and access to knowledge

The scope may change as relevant material is encountered.

## Things to Document

The project may lead to documentation about:

- Books and significant publications
- Projects and initiatives
- Organisations and institutions
- People and communities
- Events and developments
- Movements and communities

Not every subject identified through the reading will necessarily require a separate TSAP article.

## Timeline

A timeline may be developed later if the material collected provides a useful basis for one. It may include developments from early academic networking and ERNET through public Internet access, free software, Indian-language computing, digital libraries, open access, and Wikimedia.

## Sources

Sources will include books, academic publications, government and institutional documents, archived websites, digital libraries, contemporary reporting, interviews, conference proceedings, and other relevant material.

Wikipedia, Goodreads, library catalogues, and similar resources can be useful for finding books, references, and leads. Important factual claims will be checked against appropriate sources.

## Progress

The project starts with the reading list above. This page will be updated as the reading progresses and useful subjects or sources are identified.
