**OmEconomy** is an ongoing mind-mapping project that begins with an exploration of contemplation and the cultivation of focus of mind. Sustained attention and genuine mental engagement are central to contemplative practice. The exercise develops this starting point into an imagined economic model in which contemplative *japa* becomes the sole source of income, with each genuinely contemplative repetition generating ₹1. Individuals may choose any word or phrase for their japa; *Om* (ॐ) is the root word used in this particular exploration.

The mind mapping examines the mathematical, economic, social and institutional implications of this premise. It explores the relationship between focused practice, time and income, while developing questions about money creation, the recognition of qualifying repetitions, purchasing power and the organisation of economic activity.

This page documents the idea as it develops. Its equations and numerical illustrations help examine the initial assumptions and open up further lines of inquiry. It is an evolving exploration, not a completed economic theory or a practical monetary proposal.

**Mathematical preview**

The basic rule can be expressed as:

<div class="math-display">
\[
I = J_q \times ₹1
\]
</div>

If the qualifying pace is constant, the relationship between practice time and income is:

<div class="math-display">
\[
I = r \times t \times ₹1
\]
</div>

The central distinction is that income depends on qualifying contemplative repetitions, not simply on the total number of times a person repeats a word or phrase.

## Central idea

Money can be earned only through japa. A repetition counts when it is accompanied by contemplation and focus of mind; mechanical or distracted repetition does not count.

Employment, business, trade, investment and other economic activities may continue to exist, but they do not independently generate income. Money enters people's hands only through qualifying japa.

## The mathematical model

The model uses a small number of variables to express how japa generates income.

<div class="math-display">
\[
I = J_q \times ₹1
\]
</div>

Here:

- \(I\) represents the income earned.
- \(J_q\) represents the number of qualifying contemplative repetitions.
- ₹1 is the imagined income generated by each qualifying repetition.

In other words, if a person completes 10,000 qualifying repetitions, the model assigns that person an income of ₹10,000. This is the basic rule of OmEconomy, expressed mathematically.

### Connecting practice time with income

The number of qualifying repetitions can also be related to the time spent practising. If the qualifying pace remains constant during the period being considered:

<div class="math-display">
\[
J_q = r \times t
\]
</div>

Therefore:

<div class="math-display">
\[
I = r \times t \times ₹1
\]
</div>

Here, \(r\) is the number of qualifying repetitions per minute and \(t\) is the number of minutes spent practising. For example, at an illustrative pace of 60 qualifying repetitions per minute, 30 minutes of practice would generate 1,800 qualifying repetitions and therefore ₹1,800.

This is a simplifying assumption. The pace may differ between individuals and may also change during a session. The figure of 60 repetitions per minute is used for illustration, not prescribed as a universal pace.

### Total repetitions and qualifying repetitions

A crucial feature of OmEconomy is that not every attempted repetition automatically generates income. Let \(J_{\text{total}}\) represent all repetitions attempted, and let \(J_q\) represent those that meet the condition of contemplation and focus.

<div class="math-display">
\[
0 \leq J_q \leq J_{\text{total}}
\]
</div>

The inequality means that qualifying repetitions can never exceed the total repetitions attempted. If every repetition qualifies, the two quantities are equal. If some repetitions are mechanical or distracted, the number that generates income is smaller.

The model currently defines qualification conceptually: a repetition counts when it is accompanied by contemplation and focus of mind. It does not yet provide a numerical method or technical mechanism for measuring that state. No numerical qualification rate is assumed here.

## Mathematical illustrations

**Rate:** 1 qualifying japa = ₹1  
**Illustrative pace:** 60 qualifying japa per minute  
**Basis:** 30-day month; weekly figures are calculated as 7/30 of the monthly target.

### Monthly target: ₹50,000

<div class="math-display">
\[
\frac{\text{₹50,000}}{\text{₹1}} = 50{,}000\;\text{japa per month}
\]
</div>

| Period | Required japa (average) | Time at 60 japa/minute |
|:--|--:|--:|
| Month | 50,000 | 13 hr 53 min 20 sec |
| Week | ≈11,667 | ≈3 hr 14 min 27 sec |
| Day | ≈1,667 | ≈27 min 47 sec |

### Monthly target: ₹2,00,000

<div class="math-display">
\[
\frac{\text{₹2,00,000}}{\text{₹1}} = 2{,}00{,}000\;\text{japa per month}
\]
</div>

| Period | Required japa (average) | Time at 60 japa/minute |
|:--|--:|--:|
| Month | 2,00,000 | 55 hr 33 min 20 sec |
| Week | ≈46,667 | ≈12 hr 57 min 47 sec |
| Day | ≈6,667 | ≈1 hr 51 min 7 sec |

**Calculation rules**

- Monthly japa = monthly income target ÷ ₹1
- Daily average = monthly japa ÷ 30
- Weekly average = monthly japa × 7 ÷ 30
- Time in minutes = japa ÷ 60

The daily and weekly figures are averages and are rounded to the nearest whole repetition or second. The monthly totals remain exact. The pace of 60 japa per minute is an illustration for calculation; only repetitions accompanied by contemplation and focus of mind qualify.

## Mathematical assumptions and open questions

The equations above describe the basic income rule and show how the number of qualifying repetitions can be related to time. They do not, by themselves, answer every question involved in turning the idea into an economic system. Several matters remain open for exploration:

- **Issuance of money:** Who or what issues the ₹1 generated by each qualifying repetition? Is the money created by a central authority, a common pool, or another mechanism?
- **Recognition and verification:** How would a qualifying repetition be recognised? The present idea makes contemplation and focus essential, but does not specify how these would be assessed or verified.
- **Money supply:** If every person can generate money through qualifying japa, how would the total money supply change as participation and practice increase?
- **Purchasing power:** Would the purchasing power of the imagined ₹1 remain constant, or would prices and the value of money change as the system expands?

These are not resolved by the arithmetic examples. They are economic and institutional questions that the continuing mind-mapping exercise may examine. The current equations should therefore be understood as a description of the proposed earning rule, not as a complete monetary or economic theory.

## Spending

The existing world of goods and services remains in place. People can spend the money earned through japa on anything available, including food, housing, transport, education, healthcare, entertainment and other products or services. The imagined change concerns how people earn money, not what they can buy.

## History and development

The exercise begins with an interest in contemplation and the cultivation of focus of mind. OmEconomy is being explored from that starting point through an evolving mind map. This page records its current ideas; further questions and connections can be added as the exercise develops.
