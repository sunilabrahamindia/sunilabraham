# Simulation #3: Internet Governance in a Crisis

A government asks a major online platform to restrict access to material it says could trigger violence. The request is urgent, but the evidence is incomplete. A broad restriction could also block lawful speech, affect people outside the country and interfere with access to information.

You are the facilitator of a fictional multistakeholder process. Make decisions, hear objections, negotiate safeguards and see how the dispute develops. This is a rule-based teaching scenario, not a prediction of a real case.

    <h2 id="c-brief-title">The urgent blocking request</h2>
    <p>A government says a set of posts is spreading dangerous false information and asks a platform to block the material immediately. The platform has received a formal request, but the request does not clearly identify every item or explain the evidence. Some posts may be unlawful; others may be reporting, criticism or discussion.</p>
    <p><strong>Your task:</strong> help the process reach a decision that can be explained, reviewed and implemented without ignoring urgency or rights.</p>
  </section>

## How to use this case

1. Read the briefing and choose the initial response.
2. Hear how each stakeholder reacts to that decision.
3. Choose a negotiation priority and read the new issues it creates.
4. Make a final decision, then inspect the consequence indicators and decision record.
5. Restart and try a different path. Compare the trade-offs rather than looking for a single winning score.

These sources provide context for the issues in this fictional case. They do not validate the scenario's authored responses or consequence indicators.
