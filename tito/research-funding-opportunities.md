The **Research, Grants and Funding Opportunities: Information Board** is a guide to finding grants, research funding, project support, fellowships, travel assistance and related opportunities in open-source software, digital rights, Internet governance, public-interest technology and adjacent fields. It brings together official funding portals and organisations whose programmes may be relevant to researchers, contributors, community groups and public-interest projects.

Funding programmes differ in purpose, applicant requirements, geographic restrictions and application cycles. Some support individuals, while others require a registered organisation, a Wikimedia community connection, an academic affiliation, a fiscal sponsor or a project with a particular public-interest purpose. Check the official programme page before preparing an application.

This page is a time-neutral directory of funding sources, not a live list of open calls. Programme descriptions explain the general purpose of each source; they do not confirm that a programme is active, accepting applications or suitable for a particular applicant. Funding priorities, eligibility rules, application windows, budgets and programme names can change. Follow the official links and check the latest guidelines, deadlines, terms and application route before preparing a proposal or making commitments.

## Types of support covered {#support}

Funding opportunities are not all the same. Before applying, identify what kind of support is being offered and what the funder expects in return.

- **Research grants:** support for a defined research question, study, publication, dataset, tool or research team.
- **Project grants:** funding for a time-limited project, such as developing open-source software, improving digital access or carrying out public-interest technology work.
- **Core or organisational support:** funding for an organisation's ongoing work or strategic plan. This often has additional governance, financial reporting and eligibility requirements.
- **Community grants:** support for community-led activities, contributor groups, public events, documentation, outreach or participation.
- **Fellowships and residencies:** structured opportunities that may provide a stipend, mentorship, research time or access to a professional community.
- **Travel and event support:** help with travel, accommodation, registration or organising a conference. These funds may be restricted to specific events or communities.
- **Consultancies and commissioned research:** paid work delivered under a contract or terms of reference. These are not necessarily grants and may involve procurement or a competitive tender.
- **Emergency or rapid-response support:** time-sensitive assistance for people or organisations facing digital attacks, censorship or other urgent threats.

A grant is not automatically personal income. Check which costs are eligible, whether the funder pays an individual or an organisation, whether overheads and salaries are permitted, and what reporting or public outputs are required.

## Open-source software and digital commons {#open-source}

These sources may be relevant to open-source software, open standards, digital commons, open hardware, privacy-preserving technology and public-interest technical infrastructure.

- [NLnet Foundation: Apply for a grant](https://nlnet.nl/propose/) — supports technical development through funding programmes with different technical priorities and eligibility requirements. Projects may involve open-source software, open hardware, open standards or other technologies that serve an open and trustworthy Internet. Review the specific programme's scope, licensing requirements, applicant eligibility and application process before preparing a proposal.
- [Open Technology Fund (OTF): Apply](https://apply.opentech.fund/) — supports technologies and projects that advance Internet freedom. Its programmes include the Internet Freedom Fund, Rapid Response Fund and other targeted support. Each has its own purpose and process; check the current application portal.
- [GitHub Grants for Open Source in India](https://github.com/github/india/blob/main/Open%20Source/GRANTS.md) — information about support for individual open-source contributors and maintainers in India. Consult the official repository for programme scope, eligibility, application instructions and announcements about future rounds. Do not assume that a past programme is accepting applications.
- [OpenSSF](https://openssf.org/) — a source to monitor for open-source software security initiatives and community programmes. It is not a general-purpose grant directory; check each initiative for any funding or participation opportunity.
- [Linux Foundation](https://www.linuxfoundation.org/) — check official project and programme announcements for relevant mentorships, community programmes or targeted opportunities. Do not assume that every programme provides funding.
- [Open Knowledge Foundation](https://okfn.org/) — monitor official announcements for work, projects, events and opportunities related to open knowledge and data.

These organisations and programmes have different scopes. In particular, an open-source grant may prioritise maintainers, technical deliverables, public benefit or specific software ecosystems; a general research proposal may not meet those requirements.

## Digital rights and Internet freedom {#digital-rights}

Digital-rights funders may support technology development, applied research, digital security, advocacy, strategic litigation or work defending freedom of expression online. Some fund only organisations or projects that fit a defined strategy.

- [Open Technology Fund: Internet Freedom Fund](https://www.opentech.fund/funds/internet-freedom-fund/) — supports global Internet-freedom projects, including technology development, applied research, digital security and convenings. Its official page describes a two-stage application process and rolling submissions for this fund; review the current applicant guide and requirements before applying.
- [Digital Freedom Fund: Grants](https://digitalfreedomfund.org/grants/) — supports strategic litigation and related work advancing human rights in digital spaces. Consult its official grants page for its funding approach, eligibility requirements, application process and any open calls. Its focus is specialised; it should not be treated as a general-purpose research fund.
- [Internet Freedom Foundation (India)](https://internetfreedom.in/) — a digital-rights organisation to follow for research, advocacy and organisational announcements. This is a source to monitor, not a claim that it operates an open grant programme.
- [Access Now](https://www.accessnow.org/) — works on digital rights and human rights in the digital age. Monitor official announcements for relevant opportunities; do not assume it offers general project grants.
- [ARTICLE 19](https://www.article19.org/) — works on freedom of expression and information. Check official vacancies, programme announcements and calls for participation.
- [SFLC.in](https://sflc.in/) — an Indian organisation working on technology law and digital rights. Check its official website for relevant opportunities and announcements.
- [CIPESA](https://cipesa.org/) — works on digital rights and policy in Africa. Check programme-specific announcements and eligibility.

A funder's work in digital rights does not necessarily mean it accepts unsolicited proposals. Read its stated funding priorities and application process before sending a proposal.

## Internet governance and Internet infrastructure {#internet-governance}

Internet governance and infrastructure organisations sometimes offer research fellowships, participation support, training, commissioned research or project-specific funding. Many of their programmes are fellowships or professional development opportunities rather than grants.

- [Internet Society Foundation](https://www.isocfoundation.org/) — check its official grant programmes for calls related to an open, globally connected, secure and trustworthy Internet. Programme themes, eligibility and application windows vary.
- [Internet Society: Fellowship programmes](https://www.internetsociety.org/fellowships/) — a source for fellowship, mentorship and participation programmes connected with Internet development and research. Check each programme's purpose, eligibility, application process and support offered; training or travel support is not the same as a research grant.
- [Internet Governance Forum (IGF)](https://www.intgovforum.org/) — monitor official announcements for fellowship, participation, event and research-related opportunities. Calls are time-bound and may be tied to a particular meeting.
- [ICANN Fellowship Program](https://www.icann.org/fellowshipprogram/) — supports participation in the ICANN community, including mentoring and meeting-related support. Eligibility can depend on the meeting, applicant experience and programme rules. Consult the official page for application windows, selection criteria and the support available for each round.
- [APNIC](https://www.apnic.net/) — monitor official announcements for training, fellowships and community programmes related to Internet infrastructure in the Asia Pacific.
- [RIPE NCC](https://www.ripe.net/) — check official announcements for community, research, training and participation opportunities relevant to its service region.
- [ARIN](https://www.arin.net/) — monitor official announcements for programmes related to Internet number resources and technical community participation.
- [LACNIC](https://www.lacnic.net/) and [AFRINIC](https://afrinic.net/) — check the official websites for regional Internet development, training and community announcements. Verify the current status and terms of any programme.

The organisations above are not interchangeable funders. Some primarily coordinate Internet resources or convene communities; include them in a funding search only when an official programme or call is relevant to your proposed work.

## Research grants and funding organisations {#research-funding}

For research on technology, the Internet, society, policy, law, public-interest infrastructure or digital inclusion, start with the research and grant programmes of organisations whose stated aims match the proposed work.

- [Wikimedia Research Fund](https://meta.wikimedia.org/wiki/Grants:Programs/Wikimedia_Research_%26_Technology_Fund/Wikimedia_Research_Fund/en) — supports research on or about Wikimedia projects. The programme page describes support for individuals, groups and organisations, with different proposal types and requirements. It is intended for Wikimedia-related research, not general research unrelated to Wikimedia.
- [Internet Society Foundation: Research Grant Program](https://www.isocfoundation.org/grant-programme/research-grant-programme/) — supports research related to the Internet and its impact on society, subject to the programme's stated priorities and requirements. Review its research themes, collaboration expectations, eligible costs, applicant requirements and application guidance. The Foundation's other grant programmes have separate purposes and rules.
- [NLnet Foundation](https://nlnet.nl/propose/) — may be relevant when the research involves technical development and open technology. Review the requirements of the specific fund, including open licensing and any regional eligibility restrictions.
- [Open Technology Fund](https://apply.opentech.fund/) — may be relevant to applied research and technology work that fits its Internet-freedom priorities.
- [Digital Freedom Fund](https://digitalfreedomfund.org/grants/) — relevant to strategic litigation and research connected to digital rights and human rights in digital spaces.
- [Open Knowledge Foundation](https://okfn.org/) — monitor for research, open knowledge and data-related projects or calls.

Also check university research centres, academic departments, public-interest technology institutes, research councils and foundations in the relevant country or region. Their eligibility rules may require institutional affiliation, a principal investigator, a registered entity, a local partner or a specific publication or data-management plan.

The presence of an organisation on this list does not mean it currently accepts proposals. Some publish calls only periodically; others commission work or fund only selected partner organisations.

## Wikimedia Foundation funding programmes {#wikimedia}

The [Wikimedia Foundation's official funding portal](https://meta.wikimedia.org/wiki/Grants:Start/en) is particularly relevant to Wikimedia communities, contributors, affiliates, community organisers and researchers working on or about Wikimedia projects. It deserves separate attention because its programmes are designed around the Wikimedia movement and its needs, rather than being general-purpose funding for any project.

Start with the [overview of Wikimedia Foundation funding programmes](https://meta.wikimedia.org/wiki/Grants:Programs). The programme pages explain who can apply, what kinds of work are eligible, application cycles and reporting requirements. Relevant programmes include:

- **Rapid Fund:** support for short-term, lower-cost projects with a Wikimedia focus. Check the programme's eligibility rules, regional timelines and application guidance.
- **General Support Fund:** support for larger projects, programmes and annual or strategic plans connected to the Wikimedia movement. Applicants should review the organisational, financial and programme requirements.
- **Conference and Event Fund:** support for eligible events that bring Wikimedians together. Check the current event-planning guidance and application timeline.
- **Wikimedia Research Fund:** support for research on or about Wikimedia projects. Read the research programme's proposal types, eligible costs and requirements.
- **Hub Fund and other programmes:** consult the central funding portal for programme status, eligibility and application arrangements. Do not rely on an older announcement or assume every listed programme is active.

These programmes can be relevant to Wikimedia communities in India and elsewhere, but eligibility depends on the programme and applicant. A project does not qualify simply because it mentions Wikipedia or uses Wikimedia content. Applicants should explain the connection to Wikimedia projects or communities and follow the relevant programme's current guidance.

For community-led events, consult the [Wikimedia Foundation's official funding portal](https://meta.wikimedia.org/wiki/Grants:Start/en) and its programme guidance. Previously funded events may illustrate the kinds of activities supported, but past awards are not an application form or proof that a new event is eligible for funding.

## Fellowships, travel and participation support {#fellowships}

Some opportunities provide a stipend, mentoring, training or travel assistance instead of a project grant. These can be useful for researchers, policy practitioners, community members and early-career professionals, but the terms differ.

- [ICANN Fellowship Program](https://www.icann.org/fellowshipprogram/) — participation in the ICANN community and meeting-related support. Eligibility may vary by meeting and can include prior participation requirements. Check the official programme guidance for the eligibility criteria and application process for the specific round.
- [Internet Society Pulse Research Fellowship and Mentorship](https://www.internetsociety.org/fellowships/pulse-research/) — supports Internet-measurement research and related tools. Check the programme page for its current status, eligibility, application process and future rounds.
- [Internet Governance Forum (IGF)](https://www.intgovforum.org/) — check official calls for fellowships, participation and meeting-related support.
- [Outreachy](https://www.outreachy.org/) — paid, mentored internships contributing to open-source and other open projects. Check the current round, eligibility and dates.
- [Google Summer of Code](https://summerofcode.withgoogle.com/) — a stipend-supported open-source contribution programme, not employment with Google and not a guarantee of a job.
- [LFX Mentorship](https://mentorship.lfx.linuxfoundation.org/) — project-based open-source mentorship opportunities. Check each programme's schedule, eligibility and stipend terms.

A travel grant may cover only specified costs, require participation in an event, or reimburse expenses after approval. A fellowship may require a set number of hours, a research output or a report. Confirm the conditions before making travel bookings or relying on expected funding.

## Finding additional opportunities {#finding-opportunities}

A few practical methods can make funding searches more systematic.

- **Check official funding portals:** use the funder's own programme pages rather than relying solely on social posts, newsletters or third-party lists.
- **Search by the work, not only the topic:** combine terms such as research grant, project funding, community fund, open-source grant, travel support, fellowship, call for proposals and commissioned research with your subject area.
- **Check national and regional sources:** research councils, universities, public-interest technology organisations and regional networks may have calls that do not appear in global directories.
- **Subscribe to official updates:** newsletters and announcement lists can help you notice new rounds. Review eligibility and deadlines after receiving an alert.
- **Read past awards carefully:** previously funded projects can show a funder's scope, but an old award is not evidence that the same programme is open now or that another project will be eligible.
- **Track deadlines and source checks:** record the official URL, date checked, deadline and time zone, eligibility, funding type and any questions that need clarification.

Useful search phrases include:

- "open-source project grant" and "open technology funding"
- "digital rights research grant" and "Internet freedom fund"
- "Internet governance fellowship" and "Internet research funding"
- "public-interest technology grant"
- "Wikimedia Research Fund" and "Wikimedia community funding"
- "call for proposals" plus the relevant topic, country or region

Search-engine results can be stale or point to archived calls. Follow the result to the original programme page and confirm that it is current.

## Eligibility and application requirements {#eligibility}

Before writing a proposal, check the funder's eligibility rules. Common restrictions include:

- **Applicant type:** individual, informal group, registered nonprofit, university, company, Wikimedia affiliate or other organisation.
- **Geography:** country of residence, registration, project location, region served or restrictions tied to a particular funding source.
- **Institutional affiliation:** some research fellowships require an academic or research institution, supervisor or host organisation.
- **Fiscal sponsorship:** an unregistered group may need an eligible fiscal sponsor where the programme permits this arrangement.
- **Project scope:** the proposed work must fit the funder's stated themes, eligible activities and intended beneficiaries.
- **Costs and duration:** check whether salaries, equipment, travel, overheads, subcontracting and indirect costs are permitted, and whether the proposed duration fits the grant.
- **Outputs and licensing:** some programmes require open-source licences, public datasets, open publications, documentation or other public outputs.
- **Reporting and compliance:** review financial reporting, safeguarding, privacy, conflicts of interest, procurement, tax and other obligations.
- **Deadline and submission method:** check the date, time zone, required forms, supporting documents and whether the call is open.

If a rule is unclear, ask the funder through a contact address published on its official website. Do not assume eligibility based on a previous award, a social-media post or an informal conversation.

## Preparing a funding proposal {#proposals}

A clear proposal should make it easy for reviewers to understand the problem, the plan and why the work fits the funder's priorities. Requirements vary, but many applications ask for some combination of the following:

1. **Problem and public benefit:** describe the problem, who is affected and why the work matters.
2. **Fit with the funder:** connect the proposed activities to the programme's published objectives and eligibility rules.
3. **Objectives and outputs:** state what will be done and what concrete outputs or outcomes are expected.
4. **Method and work plan:** explain the approach, timeline, responsibilities and how progress will be assessed.
5. **Budget and justification:** itemise costs, explain assumptions and check that every requested cost is eligible.
6. **Team and partnerships:** describe relevant experience, roles, collaborators and any required host organisation or fiscal sponsor.
7. **Risks and sustainability:** identify important risks, privacy or safety concerns, dependencies and what happens after the grant ends.
8. **Evaluation and reporting:** explain how results will be documented and how the project will meet reporting requirements.

Do not copy a successful proposal without checking the current call. A funder's priorities, forms and requirements can change between rounds. Be accurate about experience, partners, costs and expected results, and do not promise outputs that the proposed resources cannot realistically deliver.
