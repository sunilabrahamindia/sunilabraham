**Rev. Dr. Kuruvilla Cherukara Abraham**, commonly known as **K. C. Abraham** or **KC**, was an Indian Christian theologian, pastor, educator and ecumenical leader. His work addressed liberation theology, Christian mission, ethics, ecology and the relationship between faith and social change. He held leadership and teaching roles in several church and theological institutions, including the Ecumenical Christian Centre, United Theological College in Bengaluru, the South Asia Theological Research Institute and the Ecumenical Association of Third World Theologians. He died in Bengaluru on 12 June 2016.

## Early life and education

Abraham was born in Kerala and became a pastor in the Church of South India (CSI). He began his ecumenical work as youth secretary of the CSI's Central Kerala Diocese. He later studied at United Theological College in Bengaluru, Union Theological Seminary and Princeton Theological Seminary in the United States, where he earned a PhD.

## Ministry and academic career

Abraham served as a pastor and presbyter in the CSI, including at St. Mark's Cathedral in Bengaluru from 1972 to 1979. He was also involved in research at the Christian Institute for the Study of Religion and Society (CISRS), where he worked with theologian M. M. Thomas on questions concerning religion and social realities.

From 1982 to 1986, Abraham directed the Ecumenical Christian Centre (ECC) in Whitefield, Bengaluru. During his time there, the centre developed its work in learning and social action, engaging with grassroots groups and people's movements. He subsequently served as a professor at United Theological College, Bengaluru, and held leadership responsibilities at the South Asia Theological Research Institute (SATHRI) and the Board of Theological Education of the Senate of Serampore College (BTESSC).

His academic work and teaching also took him to theological institutions outside India. He served as a visiting professor at institutions including Emmanuel College of the Toronto School of Theology, San Francisco Theological Seminary and Princeton Theological Seminary.

## Ecumenical work

Abraham was a leader of the Ecumenical Association of Third World Theologians (EATWOT), serving as its president. The association brought together theologians concerned with the relationship between Christian thought and the political, economic and cultural conditions of communities in Asia, Africa and Latin America. Abraham also participated in the work of international Christian bodies, including the World Council of Churches and the Christian Conference of Asia.

His ecumenical engagement connected theological education with questions of justice, peace, human rights and the dignity of marginalised communities. He wrote and spoke about the links between mission, ecology and social responsibility, and argued that Christian mission should involve solidarity with people experiencing poverty and oppression.

## Theological thought

A recurring concern in Abraham's writing was what he described as "liberative solidarity": the relationship between Christian faith and struggles for justice. His work challenged approaches to mission that overlooked poverty, marginalisation and ecological harm. In *Liberative Solidarity: Contemporary Perspectives on Mission*, he considered how Christian mission might respond to the social and economic conditions of the Global South.

His scholarship covered Christian ethics, theological education, pluralism, ecology, church and society, and the relationship between Christianity and social change. He also contributed to discussions of Third World theology and edited collections that brought together perspectives from theologians working in different contexts.

## Selected publications

The following titles are listed in the obituary published by the National Council of Churches in India and in the Religion Online bibliography of Abraham's writings.

- *Break Every Yoke* (1983).
- *Third World Theologies: Commonalities and Divergencies* (editor, 1991).
- *New Horizons in Ecumenism* (editor, 1993).
- *Spirituality of the Third World* (editor, 1994).
- *Liberative Solidarity: Contemporary Perspectives on Mission* (1996).
- *Christian Witness in Society: A Tribute to M. M. Thomas* (editor, 1998).

Abraham also published articles in theological journals and contributed chapters to edited volumes. His article "Globalization: A Gospel and Culture Perspective" appeared in the *International Review of Mission* in 1996.

## Personal life

In 1996, Abraham and his wife, Dr Molly Abraham, established Liza's Home in Bengaluru, a residential facility for girls and young women with disabilities. The centre was intended to support residents' dignity and participation in community life. Abraham continued to support this work alongside his theological and pastoral commitments.

## Death

Abraham died in Bengaluru on 12 June 2016. Church leaders, colleagues and former students paid tribute to his contributions to theological education, ecumenical work and public engagement with questions of justice and human dignity.
