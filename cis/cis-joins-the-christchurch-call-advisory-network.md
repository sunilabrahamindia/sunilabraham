<p>Centre for Internet & Society's application for membership of the Christchurch Call Advisory Network has been accepted! As a part of this network, we, along with other civil society groups based out of various jurisdictions, would be providing inputs on making the Call a robust, human rights-centred initiative.</p>

Many of the Advisory Network members have been engaged on the Christchurch Call since its launch and are committed to continuing to share their expertise.</p>

## Context and Background

The Christchurch Call to Action was launched on 15 May 2019 by New Zealand Prime Minister Jacinda Ardern and French President Emmanuel Macron, following the 15 March 2019 terrorist attack on two mosques in Christchurch, New Zealand. The attacker's livestreamed video of the killings drew international attention to the use of online platforms to distribute terrorist and violent extremist content. The Christchurch Call brought governments and technology companies together around commitments intended to address the spread of such content online while recognising the importance of a free, open and secure Internet.

The Christchurch Call Advisory Network (CCAN) emerged during 2019 as a civil society component of the initiative. It brought together organisations and individuals from civil society, academia and the technical community to provide independent advice on implementing the Call's commitments. Its areas of expertise included human rights, freedom of expression, digital rights, counter-radicalisation, victim support and public policy.

Against this background, the CIS news item announced that the Centre for Internet and Society had been accepted as a member of the Christchurch Call Advisory Network. The membership placed CIS within a network intended to bring civil society perspectives into discussions about how the Christchurch Call's commitments should be implemented.

CIS's participation is particularly relevant to its existing work on Internet governance, freedom of expression and human rights. Membership of the Advisory Network provided an opportunity for CIS to contribute its policy and civil society perspective to discussions concerning terrorist and violent extremist content online and the implications of measures addressing such content for an open and rights-respecting Internet.

The formation of the Advisory Network reflected an effort to include perspectives beyond governments and technology companies in the development and implementation of the Christchurch Call. Its stated purpose was to provide expert advice while considering both the need to address terrorist and violent extremist content and the protection of human rights and freedom of expression.
