**Editorial Source Audit: Fourth Stage** records the next phase of editorial work for *Essays on Dalits, Religion and Liberation II* by A. M. Abraham Ayrookuzhiel. It follows the work documented in the [first-stage Editorial Source Audit](/amaa/30-editorial-source-audit/), the [second-stage Editorial Source Audit](/amaa/30-editorial-source-audit-second-stage/) and the [third-stage Editorial Source Audit](/amaa/30-editorial-source-audit-third-stage/).

The earlier stages recorded the collection-wide capitalisation and italicisation review, comparisons with the original printed sources, heading consistency work and the development of editorial conventions. This fourth stage will take up the remaining work identified during those reviews, together with further source editing, checks and balances, style checks and other final editorial requirements.

The work will be recorded as it is undertaken. Each entry should distinguish the material examined, the issue identified, the decision taken, any source change made and the verification completed. An item must not be described as complete unless the relevant check has actually been carried out.

## Purpose and scope

This stage will provide a working record of:

- Outstanding matters carried forward from the first, second and third stages.
- Further source editing and corrections approved during the present review.
- Checks and balances across the essays and other parts of the book, including consistency, completeness and internal cross-checks.
- Further style checks and the application of the collection's established editorial conventions.
- Editorial and production-related work that affects the readiness of the book.
- Verification of changes and a record of matters that remain unresolved.

The scope is not limited to the nine essays. Where relevant, it may include the Foreword, Editor's Note, About the Author, credit page, notes, references and other parts of the book. Any work on the print-production system will be identified separately from changes to the manuscript source.

## Relationship with the earlier stages

The earlier audit pages remain the records of the work completed and decisions made in their respective stages. This fourth-stage page does not replace or retrospectively rewrite those records.

- [First stage — Capitalisation and italicisation audit](/amaa/30-editorial-source-audit/)
- [Second stage — Source verification against original publications](/amaa/30-editorial-source-audit-second-stage/)
- [Third stage — Heading consistency and Manual of Style](/amaa/30-editorial-source-audit-third-stage/)

Where a matter has already been examined, the earlier entry should be consulted. This stage will record any new review, follow-up decision or additional verification rather than duplicating the earlier account.

## Working method

For each activity, record, where applicable:

1. The essay, section, production file or other material examined.
2. The check or editorial question undertaken.
3. The finding, including any discrepancy or uncertainty.
4. The decision and its rationale.
5. The source or production change made, if authorised.
6. The verification performed and its result.
7. Any outstanding action.

Source corrections should be made only after the relevant passage has been examined in context. Avoid broad or automatic changes where individual judgement is required. Preserve the author's meaning, wording and argument unless a specific correction has been established and approved.

These were limited typographic-normalisation checks. They do not constitute a complete proofread of the essays or a verification of other sections such as publication details and notes.

**Essay 7 — lineation of the Sivavakkiyar passage**

The poem passage beginning "Who is an untouchable woman and who is a Brahman woman?" was reformatted from seven short lines into four lines. The first three lines now end at the question marks, and the concluding sentence remains on the fourth line. The existing `<div class="poem">` wrapper, wording, punctuation and footnote markup were retained. No other content in the essay was changed.

The source change was committed as `80796f66a2455e756f133956f5e5baf27cdd0a71`.

A separate four-line passage beginning "Not by birth does one become an outcaste" was reviewed for capitalisation. The decision was to retain the existing mixed capitalisation rather than capitalise every line: the lowercase beginnings of the second and third lines preserve the sentence's continuity. No change was made to that passage.

**Status:** The two punctuation-spacing corrections and the Essay 7 lineation change were committed. The relevant source sections were checked after the punctuation corrections; no print build or PDF layout verification was undertaken for the poem change.


**Essay 7 and Essay 9 — review of poem capitalisation**

Two poems in the article sources were reviewed for whether line beginnings should be capitalised uniformly:

- **Essay 7 — Dalit Theology: A Movement of Counter-Culture:** The four-line passage beginning "Not by birth does one become an outcaste" was reviewed. The existing mixed capitalisation was retained; the second and third lines begin in lowercase, preserving the continuity of the sentence structure.
- **Essay 9 — The Ideological Nature of the Emerging Dalit Consciousness:** The passage beginning "holding torch in the hands" in the poem *Dalitaru Barvaru* was reviewed in the context of the complete "Cultural Expression" section, its surrounding poems and its associated note. The lowercase "h" was retained. The poem has mixed line-initial capitalisation, including lowercase beginnings within the same composition, so capitalising this line alone would impose an inconsistent editorial convention.

**Decision:** No capitalisation changes were made to either poem. The existing capitalisation was retained in keeping with the source text and the poems' own usage. No other source content was changed as part of this review.

### 18 November 2026 — Editorial footnote and signature-spacing adjustments

**Essay 8 — editorial note for the heading "Sony!"**

The heading "Sony!" in *Dalit Liberation—Some Reflections on Their Ideological Predicament* was reviewed in the context of the essay. Its meaning could not be established from the surrounding discussion. The editorial decision was to retain the heading as it appears in the original source, rather than alter or remove it.

A numbered footnote marker was added immediately after the heading, with a corresponding entry in a new Notes section at the end of the essay. The final note reads: "The heading \"Sony!\" appears in the original source. We have retained it as printed, in fidelity to the source text." The note follows the numbered HTML footnote and return-link convention used in the other essays. The source was re-fetched and the marker and note were verified.

**Status:** Source change completed and verified. Commit: `bec1dc5a850f3f3c7352a4838e55585e61523679`.

**Print production — consistency of the Foreword and Editor's Note signatures**

The signature blocks for Dr. Y. T. Vinayaraj in the Foreword and Tito Dutta in the Editor's Note were compared in `ebook/edrl-ii/print/build.py`. The Editor's Note block had a larger leading value (9.35pt rather than 8.25pt) and an initial line break with a negative vertical adjustment, which had become unnecessary after the scanned signature was removed.

The Editor's Note signature block was adjusted to use the same 8.25pt leading and zero paragraph spacing as the Foreword signature block. The redundant initial line break and the `#v(-2pt)` adjustment were removed. The name, role and date font sizes were retained.

**Status:** Production-source adjustment completed and re-fetched for verification. Commit: `d3aaa99d944a76c03b0b30628145abb9225cbeba`. A new print build and visual PDF inspection have not yet been carried out.

### 18 November 2026 — Essay 1, subsection 6: pagination of "Will the sacred cow return?"

**Print layout intervention — moving subsection 6 to a new page**

During review of the Essay 1 print proof, the bold paragraph "6. Will the sacred cow return?" was found at the bottom of a page, with the subsection beginning in an undesirable position. This text is formatted in the manuscript as a bold paragraph, not as a semantic heading. The manuscript source was therefore left unchanged.

A targeted intervention was made in `ebook/edrl-ii/print/build.py`, in the Essay 1 processing stage, to insert a Typst weak page break immediately before the exact paragraph. The intervention is restricted to Essay 1 and to this exact text. A weak page break (`#pagebreak(weak: true)`) was chosen so that it can move the subsection to a new page when necessary without forcing an unnecessary blank page if the content is already at a page boundary.

The work required several corrective commits after the initial insertion caused failures in successive print-build runs (Builds #517–#520). The affected `md_to_typst()` function was subsequently restored from the last confirmed successful baseline, Build #516 (commit `149760f35dea1d2c8e2be181af0095427218958e`), and the page-break intervention was kept outside that function in the Essay 1 processing stage. The recovery was committed as `3d8aa8878ff5329bbac8678aa5648904b369f78b` ("Restore md_to_typst from successful build 516").

**Verification:** Build #521 (GitHub Actions run `44270980091`) completed successfully and the print-preview artifact was uploaded. The user subsequently inspected the resulting proof and confirmed that subsection 6, "Will the sacred cow return?", now appears to have moved to a new page as intended.

**Scope:** No wording or formatting was changed in the Essay 1 manuscript source. The intervention is confined to the print builder. Build #521 confirms successful generation; the pagination outcome was visually checked by the user in the generated proof.

**Status:** Print-builder intervention completed; successful build and user visual confirmation recorded.
