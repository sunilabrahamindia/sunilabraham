**Editorial Source Audit: Third Stage** records the continuation of the editorial and source-verification work for *Essays on Dalits, Religion and Liberation II* by A. M. Abraham Ayrookuzhiel. It follows the work documented in the [first-stage Editorial Source Audit](/amaa/30-editorial-source-audit/) and the [second-stage Editorial Source Audit](/amaa/30-editorial-source-audit-second-stage/).

The first stage concentrated on capitalisation, italicisation, heading treatment and quotation punctuation across the collection. The second stage continued with page-by-page comparison of the essay texts against their original printed sources, recording differences, corrections, decisions and unresolved questions.

This third stage will focus primarily on heading consistency across the nine essays and the development and application of a Manual of Style for the collection. Other editorial and source-verification matters may also be examined where they arise during the work. Findings, decisions, corrections and verification will be recorded here as the audit proceeds.

## Purpose

The purpose of this stage is to examine and document:

- Consistency in the structure, hierarchy, wording and presentation of headings across the nine essays.
- The editorial principles and conventions to be included in a Manual of Style for this collection.
- The application of agreed style decisions to the relevant essay sources.
- Other source-level or editorial questions that emerge during the review.

The audit will distinguish between an observed variation, an editorial decision, an approved source correction and a verified result. A difference will not automatically be treated as an error.

## Relationship with the first- and second-stage audits

The [first-stage audit](/amaa/30-editorial-source-audit/) remains the record of the collection-wide capitalisation and italicisation work, heading checks and quotation-punctuation review.

The [second-stage audit](/amaa/30-editorial-source-audit-second-stage/) remains the record of comparisons with the original printed publications, including transcription corrections, formatting restoration, footnote checks and unresolved source questions.

This third-stage audit does not replace or rewrite either earlier record. It will document the heading-consistency and Manual of Style work undertaken at this stage. Earlier findings will be referred to where relevant rather than duplicated.

## Scope and working method

The review will proceed across the nine essay sources. Each heading or style question will be examined in its context and in relation to the structure and editorial decisions of the collection.

For each issue, the record should identify, where applicable:

1. The essay and the precise heading, passage or source location.
2. The existing form and the consistency issue observed.
3. The relevant editorial principle or proposed style rule.
4. The decision, including any reason for retaining a variation.
5. Whether a correction was approved and applied to the source.
6. The subsequent source comparison or other verification.

Unresolved questions will remain open until there is sufficient evidence for a decision. No broad or automatic source changes will be made merely to make forms appear uniform.

## Main work areas

### 1. Heading consistency

The nine essays will be reviewed for consistency in heading hierarchy and presentation. The review may include heading levels, numbering, section labels, capitalisation, italicisation, wording patterns and the relationship between main sections and subsections.

The purpose is to establish a coherent structure across the collection while preserving meaningful differences in the organisation of individual essays. Any proposed change will be considered against the essay's argument and original source, rather than imposed mechanically.

### 2. Manual of Style

A Manual of Style will be developed from the editorial decisions and evidence gathered during the first, second and third stages. It will record the conventions adopted for this volume and provide a practical reference for applying them consistently.

The manual may address headings, capitalisation, italicisation, Indic and regional-language terms, quotations, punctuation, references, notes and other relevant editorial conventions. Each rule will be recorded with its scope, rationale and any necessary exceptions.

### 3. Other editorial and source-verification matters

Other issues may be recorded where they arise during the review. They will be assessed in context and kept distinct from the principal heading and Manual of Style work. Any correction will be documented with its evidence and verification.

## Internal alert: heading work and print-build safeguards

**The next phase will involve reviewing and, where approved, changing headings.** Heading changes may affect the print book's pagination and page count. The latest successful preview is 185 pages, compared with the earlier 183-page version. The reason for this difference has not yet been established; it must not be attributed to heading promotion without checking the generated PDF and the relevant source and template changes.

During this work, take particular care not to reintroduce website-only material into the print edition. The print build must continue to exclude the introductory lead paragraphs and the website-generated Contents list from each applicable essay, and the printed essay must begin with its actual first heading. These exclusions are part of the print pipeline; the website source text must not be rewritten merely to solve a print-layout problem.

Before accepting each revised print build, check that:

- The heading hierarchy and presentation reflect only the changes agreed for the audit.
- No website introduction, web Contents list or fragment links have appeared in the printed essay.
- No essay text has been lost, duplicated or unintentionally altered.
- All nine essays are present and the build completes successfully.
- Any change in pagination or total page count is noted and investigated rather than assumed to be an error or an expected consequence.

Keep the heading review focused. Do not make unrelated content or formatting changes while correcting heading structure. Record the change and its verification in this audit log.

## Audit log

This section will be updated as individual checks are undertaken. Each entry should distinguish what was examined, what was found, what decision was made, what source change was authorised and what remains unresolved.

## Progress

The audit is in progress. No essay or style rule will be marked complete until the relevant review, decision and verification have been recorded.

**Working principle: Inspect the source → identify the consistency question → establish the editorial rule → record the decision → make an approved correction → verify the result.**
