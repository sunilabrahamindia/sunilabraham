**Editorial Source Audit: Third Stage** records the continuation of the editorial and source-verification work for *Essays on Dalits, Religion and Liberation II* by A. M. Abraham Ayrookuzhiel. It follows the work documented in the [first-stage Editorial Source Audit](/amaa/30-editorial-source-audit/) and the [second-stage Editorial Source Audit](/amaa/30-editorial-source-audit-second-stage/).

The first stage concentrated on capitalisation, italicisation, heading treatment and quotation punctuation across the collection. The second stage continued with page-by-page comparison of the essay texts against their original printed sources, recording differences, corrections, decisions and unresolved questions.

This third stage will focus primarily on heading consistency across the nine essays and the development and application of a Manual of Style for the collection. Other editorial and source-verification matters may also be examined where they arise during the work. Findings, decisions, corrections and verification will be recorded here as the audit proceeds.

## Purpose

The purpose of this stage is to examine and document:

- Consistency in the structure, hierarchy, wording and presentation of headings across the nine essays.
- The editorial principles and conventions to be included in a Manual of Style for this collection.
- The application of agreed style decisions to the relevant essay sources.
- Other source-level or editorial questions that emerge during the review.

The audit will distinguish between an observed variation, an editorial decision, an approved source correction and a verified result. A difference will not automatically be treated as an error.

## Relationship with the first- and second-stage audits

The [first-stage audit](/amaa/30-editorial-source-audit/) remains the record of the collection-wide capitalisation and italicisation work, heading checks and quotation-punctuation review.

The [second-stage audit](/amaa/30-editorial-source-audit-second-stage/) remains the record of comparisons with the original printed publications, including transcription corrections, formatting restoration, footnote checks and unresolved source questions.

This third-stage audit does not replace or rewrite either earlier record. It will document the heading-consistency and Manual of Style work undertaken at this stage. Earlier findings will be referred to where relevant rather than duplicated.

## Scope and working method

The review will proceed across the nine essay sources. Each heading or style question will be examined in its context and in relation to the structure and editorial decisions of the collection.

For each issue, the record should identify, where applicable:

1. The essay and the precise heading, passage or source location.
2. The existing form and the consistency issue observed.
3. The relevant editorial principle or proposed style rule.
4. The decision, including any reason for retaining a variation.
5. Whether a correction was approved and applied to the source.
6. The subsequent source comparison or other verification.

Unresolved questions will remain open until there is sufficient evidence for a decision. No broad or automatic source changes will be made merely to make forms appear uniform.

## Main work areas

### 1. Heading consistency

The nine essays will be reviewed for consistency in heading hierarchy and presentation. The review may include heading levels, numbering, section labels, capitalisation, italicisation, wording patterns and the relationship between main sections and subsections.

The purpose is to establish a coherent structure across the collection while preserving meaningful differences in the organisation of individual essays. Any proposed change will be considered against the essay's argument and original source, rather than imposed mechanically.

### 2. Manual of Style

A Manual of Style will be developed from the editorial decisions and evidence gathered during the first, second and third stages. It will record the conventions adopted for this volume and provide a practical reference for applying them consistently.

The manual may address headings, capitalisation, italicisation, Indic and regional-language terms, quotations, punctuation, references, notes and other relevant editorial conventions. Each rule will be recorded with its scope, rationale and any necessary exceptions.

### Important checklist

**Italicisation in headings:** Italicise only the Indian-language words and expressions in a heading. Keep English words in roman type, even when they appear beside an italicised Indian word. For example, write *Gangamma* festival, not *Gangamma festival*. If a heading consists entirely of an Indian-language word, italicise the whole word (for example, *Papam* or *Punyam*).

Apply this rule consistently across all nine essays. Do not italicise an entire mixed-language heading merely because it contains an Indian-language term.

### 3. Other editorial and source-verification matters

Other issues may be recorded where they arise during the review. They will be assessed in context and kept distinct from the principal heading and Manual of Style work. Any correction will be documented with its evidence and verification.

## Print heading normalisation — GitHub issue #63

A separate print-production issue concerning heading hierarchy was investigated and corrected under [GitHub issue #63](https://github.com/sunilabrahamindia/sunilabraham/issues/63). The problem arose because Markdown headings inside a `## Full text` wrapper were promoted when the wrapper was removed for print, but HTML headings were processed separately and retained their original levels.

The correction was made only in `ebook/edrl-ii/print/build.py`. It applies to the following four wrapper-based essays:

1. [The Church and the Study of Religion](/amaa/the-church-and-the-study-of-religion/)
2. [Religions and the Aspirations of the People](/amaa/religions-and-the-aspirations-of-the-people/)
3. [Dalit Liberation—Some Reflections on Their Ideological Predicament](/amaa/dalit-liberation-some-reflections-on-their-ideological-predicament/)
4. [The Ideological Nature of the Emerging Dalit Consciousness](/amaa/the-ideological-nature-of-the-emerging-dalit-consciousness/)

For these four essays, the print extraction promotes headings by one level when the wrapper is removed: H2 remains H2, H3 becomes H2, H4 becomes H3, H5 becomes H4 and H6 becomes H5. The essay title remains H1. The rule applies to both Markdown ATX headings and HTML heading tags.

The other five essays, which have separate full-text source pages, are unaffected. No original essay source files and no editorial/e-book build files were changed.

**Print-proof check:** Workflow run #436 completed successfully. The comparison recorded in issue #63 showed "The Secular Option" and "The Structural factor in Religion" at the same heading level and with matching visual hierarchy. The issue documents the implementation and proof observation.

This is a print-pipeline correction, not an instruction to change the source heading levels in these four essays during the present editorial heading audit. The source-level review and decisions for each essay remain separately recorded below.

## Internal alert: heading work and print-build safeguards

**The next phase will involve reviewing and, where approved, changing headings.** Heading changes may affect the print book's pagination and page count. The latest successful preview is 185 pages, compared with the earlier 183-page version. The reason for this difference has not yet been established; it must not be attributed to heading promotion without checking the generated PDF and the relevant source and template changes.

During this work, take particular care not to reintroduce website-only material into the print edition. The print build must continue to exclude the introductory lead paragraphs and the website-generated Contents list from each applicable essay, and the printed essay must begin with its actual first heading. These exclusions are part of the print pipeline; the website source text must not be rewritten merely to solve a print-layout problem.

Before accepting each revised print build, check that:

- The heading hierarchy and presentation reflect only the changes agreed for the audit.
- No website introduction, web Contents list or fragment links have appeared in the printed essay.
- No essay text has been lost, duplicated or unintentionally altered.
- All nine essays are present and the build completes successfully.
- Any change in pagination or total page count is noted and investigated rather than assumed to be an error or an expected consequence.

Keep the heading review focused. Do not make unrelated content or formatting changes while correcting heading structure. Record the change and its verification in this audit log.

### Essay 6 — Religion: A Way of Salvation or an Ideology of Oppression?

**Source examined:** `amaa/religion-way-salvation-ideology-oppression-full-text.md`.

**Heading hierarchy review**

- Reviewed the Markdown heading hierarchy from H2 to H5, working from the deepest level upwards.
- The five festival labels originally marked as H5 headings — "(a) Makara Sankranti", "(a) Sri Ramanavami", "(b) Naga Panchami", "(c) Dasara" and "(d) Deepavali" — were judged to function as parallel festival labels rather than structural section headings.
- These labels were converted in the article source to paragraph labels. The four labels under subsection B were subsequently brought into the same source treatment as the five labels under subsection A.
- The H4 subsection headings A, B and C remain headings. The festival labels are not promoted or retained as heading levels.
- The four H3 rites-of-passage headings were also corrected to follow the Indian-word-only italicisation rule:
  - "(a) Birth and naming of the Child" — removed italics from the entirely English heading.
  - "(b) *Mudi* (Hair-cutting)" — retained italics only for the Indian-language word *Mudi*.
  - "(c) Marriage" — removed italics from the entirely English heading.
  - "(d) Death" — removed italics from the entirely English heading.
- These were four targeted source corrections. The heading wording and hierarchy were otherwise retained.

**Section A and Section B label treatment**

Section A, "Prostitution of Autochthonous Festivals by the Caste Society", contains five parallel labels:

1. (a) *Makara Sankranti*
2. (b) *Agate Male Ungalu* (*Hasta Malai*)
3. (c) *Gangamma* festival
4. (d) *Sapplamma* festival
5. (e) An egalitarian spiritual practice during the *Karaga*

Section B, "Greater Exclusion of Holeyas and Madigas from Brahminical Festivals", contains four parallel labels:

1. (a) *Sri Ramanavami*
2. (b) *Naga Panchami*
3. (c) *Dasara*
4. (d) *Deepavali*

All nine labels are bold paragraph labels in the article source. Indian-language names and expressions are italicised; English words remain roman. The body paragraphs following the labels were not rewritten as part of this formatting work.

**Print-rendering issue and correction**

The first print proof showed inconsistent indentation between the two sets of labels. The cause was in the print conversion: Section A's five labels were explicitly converted into Typst paragraph blocks with a zero first-line indent, whereas Section B initially had a separate special rule for only its first label. The remaining three Section B labels were left to ordinary Markdown paragraph rendering and inherited different indentation.

Several unsuccessful print runs followed while this difference was being addressed. Runs #452 and #453 failed because the special Section B matching rule did not match the source text. Runs #454 to #457 completed, but the indentation inconsistency remained. These attempts exposed the underlying problem: Section B needed the same rendering rule as Section A, not a separately guessed indentation adjustment for its first label.

The final correction was confined to `ebook/edrl-ii/print/build.py`:

- Removed the separate special-case rule for Section B label (a).
- Added all four Section B labels to the existing list of festival labels handled by the Section A rule.
- Both sets of labels now pass through the same explicit Typst paragraph-block treatment, including `first-line-indent: 0pt`.
- No global paragraph settings, unrelated print rules or article wording were changed in this correction.

**H2 print treatment**

A short ornamental line beneath H2 headings was briefly tested in the print builder. After review, it was rejected and reverted. The original H2 typography and spacing were restored; the final print builder contains no ornamental line beneath H2 headings. The experiment and its reversal did not alter the essay source. No build was triggered for this experiment or its revert.

**Build and verification**

- Print workflow #458 completed successfully and generated the artifact `edrl-ii-print-preview-458`.
- The shared festival-label rendering correction was included in that successful build.
- The subsequent H3 italicisation corrections were source-only changes and did not require a print-builder change or trigger a build.
- The user has now confirmed that Essay 6 is complete.

**Status:** Essay 6's heading review and approved source corrections are complete. The nine festival labels are consistently treated as bold paragraph labels in Sections A and B, with a shared print-rendering rule. The four H3 rites-of-passage headings follow the Indian-word-only italicisation rule. The H2 ornamental-line experiment has been reverted, leaving the original print styling in place.
