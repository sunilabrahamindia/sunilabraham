**Word Frequency Analysis and Subject Index Research** is a working exercise for preparing the subject index of *Essays on Dalits, Religion and Liberation II* by A. M. Abraham Ayrookuzhiel. The exercise covers the nine main essays only. Its purpose is to use computational frequency analysis to discover potentially important terms and then examine and select them manually for the book's scholarly subject index.

The central principle is that frequency analysis will help identify candidates; editorial judgement will determine the final index.

## 1. Source material

The analysis covers these nine essays:

1. State of Hinduism in 2000 AD
2. Religion, Spirituality and Aspirations of the People
3. Living Hindu Popular Religious Consciousness
4. The Church and the Study of Religion
5. Religions and the Aspirations of the People
6. Religion: Way of Salvation or Ideology of Oppression?
7. Dalit Theology Movement: Counter-Culture
8. Dalit Liberation: Some Reflections on Their Ideological Predicament
9. The Ideological Nature of the Emerging Dalit Consciousness

The foreword, editor's note, acknowledgements, author's note and all other preliminary matter are excluded.

## 2. Word frequency analysis

The first exercise is to extract the text of all nine essays and generate frequency lists.

The analysis will produce:

- **Combined frequency list:** Meaningful words and terms ranked by frequency across all nine essays.
- **Essay-wise frequency lists:** Separate rankings for each essay, so that the distinctive subjects of individual essays are not lost in the combined results.
- **Consolidated terms:** Related forms and variations examined together where appropriate, without automatically treating different concepts as identical.

Common grammatical words and non-substantive terms will be excluded.

## 4. Manual subject selection

Frequency alone will not determine whether a term belongs in the index.

Each candidate will be examined in its essay context. The review will consider:

- Whether the subject is substantively discussed.
- Whether it represents an important argument, concept or historical reference.
- Whether it is significant to the overall collection or to an individual essay.
- Whether a reader might reasonably look it up in the index.
- Whether it should be a main entry, a subentry or a cross-reference.

Important subjects that occur infrequently must not be overlooked. Frequently occurring but unimportant words must not be included merely because of their counts.

## 5. Candidate review and selection

The candidate list will be reviewed manually and classified as:

- **Include:** Significant subjects suitable for the final index.
- **Review:** Terms requiring closer examination of their context.
- **Exclude:** Incidental mentions, generic words and terms without sufficient indexing value.

Personal names will be standardised in surname-first order wherever applicable. Names of institutions and organisations will be retained in their recognised forms.

## 6. Final subject index

The selected subjects will be organised into a concise scholarly index.

The final index will include meaningful subjects, important human names, communities, movements, organisations and institutions. Subentries and cross-references will be used only where they improve navigation.

Every page reference will be checked against the printed page numbers of the nine main essays in the final paginated proof.

**Editorial observations**

The following subjects appear particularly important to the essay's central arguments:

1. Secularisation
2. Sakti
3. Concept of God
4. Religion and meaning in life
5. God of play and God of history
6. Religious reinterpretation and social values
7. Scientific temper
8. Sacred cow and the utilitarian view of nature

"Secularisation" and "Secularism" are related but not interchangeable. The essay discusses both the process of secularisation and the secular option as a worldview. Their treatment as separate index entries remains a decision for review.

Ambedkar does not appear in this essay and is therefore not included as a candidate. Individual deities, traditional terms and examples have not automatically been made separate subject entries.

## 9. Completion criteria

The exercise will be considered complete when the frequency results have been generated, the candidate terms have been manually reviewed, and the selected entries have been checked against the essays.

The resulting subject index will be integrated into the paperback edition of *Essays on Dalits, Religion and Liberation II*.

**Working principle: Computational analysis discovers; manual editorial review selects; page-by-page verification confirms.**
