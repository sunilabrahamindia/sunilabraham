**Editorial Source Audit** documents the review of capitalisation and italicisation in the nine essays included in *Essays on Dalits, Religion and Liberation II* by A. M. Abraham Ayrookuzhiel. The audit responds to the editorial review of the book, which identified inconsistencies in the treatment of terms such as "Dalit" and "Sakti/sakti". These variations may occur within an individual essay as well as across the collection.

The purpose is to establish a consistent and defensible editorial approach while preserving the author's meaning, terminology and argument. This is a source-level exercise: decisions and approved corrections will be made in the individual essay source files, not through `build.py`, the Typst layout or an automated typesetting rule. The decisions will also be recorded in an editorial style-sheet that can guide the preparation of other works by the author.

## Scope and editorial approach

The audit covers the nine main essays listed below. It does not cover the Foreword, Editor's Note, acknowledgements or other preliminary matter.

Two distinct aspects will be examined:

1. **Capitalisation:** Consistency in the use of uppercase and lowercase forms of Indic names and terms. Examples include "Dalit/dalit", "Dalits/dalits" and "Deva/deva". The grammatical and contextual use of each term must be considered; a mechanical replacement is not sufficient.
2. **Italicisation:** Consistency in italicising Indic words and expressions. "Sakti/sakti" is an initial example. Other terms will be added as they are identified in the essays.

Capitalisation and italicisation will be audited separately. A decision about the capitalisation of a word does not automatically determine whether it should be italicised.

## The nine essays

The audit will follow the book's essay order:

1. **The State of Hinduism in 2000 A.D.—Some Emerging Trends**
2. **Religion, Spirituality and Aspirations of the People**
3. **The Living Hindu Popular Religious Consciousness and Some Reflections on It in the Context of Hindu-Christian Dialogue**
4. **The Church and the Study of Religion**
5. **Religions and the Aspirations of the People**
6. **Religion: A Way of Salvation or an Ideology of Oppression?**
7. **Dalit Theology: A Movement of Counter-Culture**
8. **Dalit Liberation — Some Reflections on Their Ideological Predicament**
9. **The Ideological Nature of the Emerging Dalit Consciousness**

## Audit procedure

The review will begin with one essay at a time so that the editorial rules can be tested against actual usage and context. Once the approach is sufficiently clear, the work can proceed two essays at a time.

For each essay:

1. Examine the source text and identify relevant terms and their variations in spelling, capitalisation and italicisation.
2. Read each occurrence in context. Consider whether the word is a name, a common noun, an adjective, a quoted expression or part of a particular theological or philosophical usage.
3. Compare usage within the essay and, where relevant, with decisions already made for the other essays.
4. Make an editorial judgement: standardise the form, retain a contextually justified variation, or defer the decision for further examination.
5. Record the decision and the preferred form in the working style-sheet.
6. Only after the decision is clear, make the approved correction in the essay's source file.
7. Recheck the affected passages to ensure that the correction has not altered the meaning or introduced a new inconsistency.

No bulk find-and-replace operation will be used before the relevant editorial rule has been established. Where a variation may reflect the original printed source, that possibility will be noted and considered rather than silently treated as a transcription error.

## Audit A: Capitalisation

The capitalisation review will begin with "Dalit/dalit" and "Dalits/dalits", followed by "Deva/deva" and other Indic terms identified during the essay-wise examination.

The review will consider:

- Whether a term is used as a proper name, a common noun or an adjective.
- Whether uppercase and lowercase forms carry a contextual or grammatical distinction.
- Whether the same usage is followed consistently within the essay.
- Whether the form agrees with the editorial decisions adopted elsewhere in the collection.
- Whether a variation appears to come from the original source text and requires a considered editorial decision.

The objective is not to impose capitalisation mechanically, but to adopt consistent usage wherever the context permits.

## Audit B: Italicisation

The italicisation review will begin with "Sakti/sakti" and continue with other Indic words and expressions found in the essays.

The review will consider:

- Whether the same word is italicised consistently within an essay.
- Whether the treatment is consistent across all nine essays.
- Whether a word is being used as an Indic term or as an established part of the surrounding English text.
- Whether the word appears in a quotation, title or other context that affects its treatment.
- Whether the source's existing italicisation should be retained or standardised.

The audit will record the preferred treatment for each term in the editorial style-sheet before approved source corrections are made.

## Editorial decision record and style-sheet

The style-sheet will be built from decisions made during the audit, rather than written in advance of examining the essays. For each term or expression, the record will capture:

- The term and its observed variants.
- The essay and the context in which it occurs.
- The capitalisation decision.
- The italicisation decision.
- The preferred editorial form, where standardisation is appropriate.
- Any qualification or exception.
- Whether the decision has been applied to the source file and checked.

The style-sheet will serve as the reference for maintaining consistency across this volume and, where applicable, for editing other works by A. M. Abraham Ayrookuzhiel.

## Progress

The audit will be recorded essay by essay. An essay will be marked complete only after both capitalisation and italicisation have been examined, the relevant decisions have been recorded, and approved source corrections have been checked.

The two audits remain distinct workstreams. A term may be consistent in its capitalisation but inconsistent in its italicisation, or vice versa.

**Working principle: Examine in context → make an editorial judgement → record the rule → correct the source → verify the result.**
