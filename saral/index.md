**Saral Kumar Chatterji** (also published as **Saral K. Chatterji**, 1932–2002) was an Indian Christian social thinker, editor and academic. He served as director of the Christian Institute for the Study of Religion and Society (CISRS) from 1976 until his death. Earlier, he was principal of Serampore College, where he established the William Carey Study and Research Center. Through CISRS, he connected Christian theological reflection with public affairs and social action, addressing caste, social justice, the rights of Dalit and Adivasi communities, and women's rights. His publications covered Indian politics, constitutional rights, modernisation, and society and culture in North-East India.

## Early career

Library authority records give Chatterji's birth year as 1932 and associate him with both CISRS and Serampore College. According to the CISRS institutional biography, he joined the institute in 1969 as associate director, based in New Delhi. His work there included organising seminars and research activities concerning political science, economics, sociology and party politics.

In 1972, Chatterji moved to Serampore to serve as principal of Serampore College. While there, he established the William Carey Study and Research Center (WCSRC), with M. M. Thomas as its chairperson. The centre subsequently collaborated with CISRS in programmes and academic research.

## Leadership at CISRS

Chatterji became director of CISRS in 1976 and remained in the role until 2002. The institute's account describes his leadership as a period in which social action became central to its programmes. CISRS connected theological research with public concerns and encouraged churches to engage with the experiences and struggles of marginalised communities.

Programmes associated with this period included community organisation, theology and development studies, fellowships focused on justice concerns, and the Delhi Forum for Christian Concern for People's Struggles. CISRS also worked alongside organisations and initiatives addressing women's rights, Dalit Christian concerns and access to legal support. The Joint Women's Programme (JWP), the All India Christian People's Forum (AICPF), the All India Dalit Women's Forum (AIDWF) and the Legal Education and Aid Society (LEAS) are among the initiatives described in the institute's account.

The CISRS biography also records the role of Jyotsna Chatterji, Saral Chatterji's wife, in the development of women's studies and action programmes. These initiatives provided platforms for women, including those from disadvantaged communities, to organise around their rights and needs.

## Theology and social engagement

Chatterji's writing and editorial work explored the relationship between Christian thought, politics and social change. Later institutional writing about CISRS identifies him as one of the contributors to its journal *Religion and Society* and to theological discussion about caste, Dalit concerns and the experiences of marginalised communities.

His article "Reflections on Theology of the People" appeared in *Religion and Society*, volume 44, number 4, in December 1997. The article is cited in a later CISRS discussion of the institute's contribution to Dalit studies.

His published work also addressed Indian political institutions, constitutional rights, elections, the Emergency, modernisation in Asia, and society and culture in North-East India. The range of these subjects reflects his engagement with both public affairs and Christian social thought.

## Publications

Library catalogues attribute the following books and edited works to Chatterji. Dates below are catalogue publication dates; where the catalogue identifies an edited volume or co-authored work, that role is noted.

- *The People, the Parliament, and the Fundamental Rights* (1969).
- *Legalisation of Abortion* (1971).
- *Political Prospects in India: A Post-Election Enquiry* (1971).
- *The Asian Meaning of Modernization: East Asia Christian Conference Studies* (1972), with M. Abel.
- *Profile of Bangla Desh: A Depth Study of a Social Revolution* (1972).
- *The Coalition Government: A Critical Examination of the Concept of Coalition, the Performance of Some Coalition Governments, and the Future Prospects of Coalition in India* (1974), with Omaprakāśa Dīpaka.
- *The Meaning of the Indian Experience: The Emergency* (1978), editor.
- *Essays in Celebration of the CISRS Silver Jubilee* (1983), editor.
- *Bipin Chandra Pal* (1984).
- *Society and Culture in North-East India: A Christian Perspective* (1996), editor.

This list is based on library catalogues and is not intended to be an exhaustive bibliography. Catalogue records sometimes vary in how they represent author, editor and contributor roles.

## Death

Chatterji died on 8 December 2002 at his residence in New Delhi. A report published by *Dalit Voice* in January 2003 described his age as 70 and highlighted his work with CISRS on Dalit and Adivasi concerns. The CISRS institutional biography also records his death in 2002.
