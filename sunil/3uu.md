The **Triple Under Utilisation** theory, conceived by [Sunil Abraham](/sunil/), is an emerging framework that examines how societies make limited use of three existing resources: computers and digital devices, human capability and labour, and gratis or free artificial intelligence systems and services. It considers these forms of underutilisation interconnected, leaving technological and human potential underused across social, economic, educational, and creative activities despite the widespread availability of digital infrastructure.

The theory explores whether collaboration and solidarity among technological institutions, economic elites, skilled professionals, and disadvantaged communities could help put this existing capacity to better use. It also considers how freely available digital systems, including smartphones, internet infrastructure, open-source technologies, and AI tools, may enable individuals and small groups to undertake work and knowledge production that previously required substantial institutional or financial resources.

## Background

The theory starts from the observation that access to digital technologies does not always translate into their broader productive or intellectual use. Although billions of people possess mobile phones and internet-connected devices, many use them mainly for communication or entertainment rather than for education, publishing, documentation, research, or collaborative production.

Human capability is also structurally underutilised, particularly among poor and marginalised populations. Skills, local knowledge, labour, creativity, oral histories, cultural memory, and lived experience often remain undocumented or economically unsupported despite their potential social value.

The framework also examines the rapid expansion of gratis and free AI systems, many of which can assist with writing, translation, accessibility, research, education, organisation, coding, and media production.

## Core Idea

The theory asks whether existing infrastructure, technologies, networks, and human capacities can be used more effectively and equitably, rather than focusing exclusively on creating entirely new systems.

It explores models of cooperation that combine access to technological capability with human participation, local knowledge, and collaborative production. Such approaches may include:

- Smartphone-first publishing
- AI-assisted knowledge production
- Collaborative archival systems
- Open and reproducible publishing workflows
- Community-led documentation projects
- Shared digital infrastructure
- Human-AI cooperative work models
- Low-cost and decentralised publishing systems

## Human Flourishing

The framework treats maximisation of utility not merely as economic efficiency, but as a process connected to human flourishing across social, educational, intellectual, cultural, and economic dimensions.

It also examines barriers that limit participation, including inequality, digital illiteracy, language exclusion, social hierarchy, unequal access to networks, institutional distrust, and uneven technological literacy.

## Demonstration Projects

The framework may be explored through practical demonstrations, experimental publishing systems, collaborative archives, and community-based digital projects developed within the broader Triple Under Utilisation approach.
