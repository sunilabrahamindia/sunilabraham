***Re-imagining Dalit Theology: Postmodern Readings*** is a book by Indian theologian Y. T. Vinayaraj, published by Christava Sahitya Samithi (CSS) in Thiruvalla in 2008. The title and publication details are recorded in Vinayaraj's curriculum vitae. Academic writing on Dalit theology also cites the book in discussions of subalternity and theological method.

## Background

Dalit theology developed in response to the exclusion and marginalisation of Dalit experiences within dominant social and religious frameworks. In his later chapter "Envisioning a Postmodern Method of Doing Dalit Theology", Vinayaraj describes Dalit theology as a challenge to caste-based systems of knowledge, while also questioning whether some established approaches to theology remain tied to assumptions associated with modernity. He argues for reconsidering the methods used to develop Dalit theology in a postmodern context.

That chapter appeared in the edited collection *Dalit Theology in the Twenty-First Century: Discordant Voices, Discerning Pathways*. It is relevant to the intellectual context of Vinayaraj's work, but it is a separate publication and is not presented here as a chapter from the 2008 book.

## Themes

The available academic evidence points to the relationship between Dalit theology, postmodern thought and subalternity as important areas for understanding this book. A doctoral thesis, *Towards a Subaltern Public Theology for India*, cites *Re-imagining Dalit Theology: Postmodern Readings* and discusses Vinayaraj's treatment of subalternity, including the ways marginalised people can be positioned as recipients of knowledge defined by dominant structures.

Read in this context, the book's stated project of "re-imagining" Dalit theology concerns not only the experiences represented in theology but also the methods and assumptions through which theological knowledge is produced. This is a cautious thematic account based on the book's title and later academic discussion that cites it; a complete text or verified table of contents was not available to confirm the book's full argument chapter by chapter.
